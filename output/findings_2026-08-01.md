# Findings — 2026-08-01

Four investigations closed. **Nothing was added to the board.** The pre-registered
bar for promoting the dog-money signal was n >= 100 dog games and a bootstrap CI
lower bound above zero with the execution haircut applied. It failed on both, and
then failed two further tests it was not required to pass.

---

## 1. The +40% dog signal — NOT promoted

Sample grew from 29 games to **56** by dropping the picks-file dependency and
using every game Kalshi has settled (856 usable, up from 343). Four independent
reasons it does not go live:

**a. It is a spike, not a plateau.** The threshold sweep was pre-registered as
the shape test, and the shape is wrong:

| min share | ROI (1c haircut) | n |
|---|---|---|
| 55% | -11.9% | 177 |
| 60% | -0.9% | 125 |
| 65% | +8.8% | 82 |
| **70%** | **+23.0%** | **56** |
| 75% | +9.8% | 30 |
| 80% | -33.3% | 9 |

A real effect degrades gracefully either side of its optimum. This one is
negative at 55%, peaks hard at 70%, and inverts at 80%. That is the signature of
fitting noise, and 70% was the threshold the original 29-game result came from —
so the peak is where we went looking.

**b. The CI still includes zero.** -3.7% to +13.7%, day-block bootstrap.
p = 0.106 against the market-calibrated null.

**c. n = 56, against a pre-registered bar of 100.**

**d. The better measurement disconfirms it outright** — see below. This is the
one that settles it.

## 2. Taker flow — the cleaner measurement kills the finding

Volume counts both sides of every trade, so it measures interest, not conviction.
Taker notional (who crossed the spread) is the direct read. On 351 games with
complete trade history (2150 fetches, zero failures):

| | n | ROI |
|---|---|---|
| back the more aggressive-money side | 351 | +2.8% |
| at 70%+ lopsided | 188 | -2.9% |
| **dog subset** | **17** | **-8.3%** |

p = 0.411, CI -13.1% to +7.7%. **The dog effect does not replicate.** When money
is measured properly rather than by proxy, backing the dog with more aggressive
flow *loses*. Two measurements of the same underlying quantity disagreeing, with
the better one showing nothing, is the strongest available evidence that the
volume result was an artifact.

## 3. Source agreement — confirms the existing rule, adds nothing new

Eight side-reads normalised to away/home across 392 games. The structural
finding is consistent and one-directional: **the crowd of sources beats any lone
dissenter.**

| lone dissenter | n | back it | fade it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `covers` | 60 | -22.4% | +10.1% |
| `polymarket_bets` | 69 | -25.4% | +18.8% |
| `kalshi_money` | 75 | -19.0% | +8.4% |
| `line` | 91 | -15.6% | +6.7% |
| `vegas` | 222 | -13.7% | +3.8% |
| `pm_book` | 71 | -3.0% | -6.3% |
| `vsin_bets` | 63 | +3.3% | -11.9% |
| `forum` | 94 | +9.2% | -14.9% |

Six of eight sources lose when standing alone. This is what the live consensus
rule already does, so it is corroboration rather than a new edge.

**Why "back the majority when split" is not shipped despite +4.7% (n=314):**
in-sample +8.0% (n=217), holdout **-2.8%** (n=97). Textbook decay.

Also notable and counter-intuitive: when all sources agree, backing them returns
**-11.5%** (n=46). Unanimity appears to be a *warning*, not a green light —
consistent with everything else this season, since agreement means the price has
already absorbed it. Too few holdout games (n=1) to act on.

## 4. Prop records

| | record | win% | units | ROI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Singles | 29-18 | 61.7% | -3.84u | **-8.2%** |
| Parlays | 13-34 | 27.7% | -16.21u | **-34.5%** |

Singles hit 61.7% but average ~-200, where breakeven is 66.7% — the hit rate is
real and the price still eats it. Only the -250..-200 bucket is neutral
(+0.1%, n=20); every other band loses.

Parlays are the worst-performing component in the system, losing on 7 of 9 days.
That is structural, not variance: compounding legs that are individually -8% EV
produces exactly this. **Recommend retiring parlays** rather than tuning them.
Left running for now since they are backend-only with no money at risk — the call
is yours.

## 5. Confirmation source — live rule VALIDATED, no change made

Tested whether Kalshi could replace Polymarket as the confirming venue, since
PM covers 146 of 295 qualifying games against Kalshi's 256. Higher coverage
would mean more plays per day. It does not survive:

| confirmation (line-against required) | all-time | in-sample | holdout |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pm` (live) | +28.3% (n=17) | +34.9% | **+23.8%** (n=10) |
| `kalshi` | +10.4% (n=50) | +18.4% | **-5.1%** (n=17) |
| `either` | +12.3% (n=54) | +22.1% | **-5.6%** (n=19) |
| `none` | +11.5% (n=68) | +23.2% | **-14.6%** (n=21) |

**Kalshi confirmation decays out of sample; Polymarket's does not.** The
head-to-head agrees: on the 58 games where the venues conflict, following
Kalshi's read returns -6.9% (n=43) and Polymarket's -1.3% (n=15). Kalshi has
more data and worse data. Rejected.

Two things this incidentally validates:

- **Confirmation is doing real work.** Removing it entirely (`none`) holds up
  in-sample at +23.2% and collapses to **-14.6%** in holdout. The gate is not
  decoration.
- **The line-against filter earns its keep.** Under `pm`, with the filter:
  holdout +23.8%. Without it: holdout +5.5% (n=54). It costs volume but roughly
  quadruples the holdout return - the call to add it was right.

### The real cost, and the one open option

The live rule finds **0.4 plays per slate day**. That is the honest reason for
empty boards - not a bug, and not missing data, but a genuinely strict rule.

The best higher-volume alternative is `both` venues confirming with **no** line
filter: all-time +8.4% (n=81, **2.1/day**), holdout **+10.3%** (n=46). It carries
5x the plays and 4.6x the holdout sample of the live rule, at a lower headline
ROI.

**Not switched**, because the pre-registered bar was "beats the live rule on the
holdout AND all-time", and +8.4% does not beat +28.3% all-time. But that
comparison rests on n=17 vs n=81, so it is not a confident ranking - and this
cell was picked from a 10-cell matrix, so it carries a selection penalty. It is
a preference call between few high-conviction plays and more moderate ones,
which is the user's to make, not a statistical one to settle here.
