# Has the market been right about Philadelphia Phillies?

_Each game's two prices are de-vigged into probabilities summing to one; the team's own share is what the market claimed. Add those up across every graded game and you get the wins the market expected — then compare to the wins they got._

- graded games with both prices: **82**

## All season

| games | market expected | actual wins | gap | flat-stake |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 82 | 46.3 | 44 | **-2.3** | -8.29u (-10.1%) |

- re-simulating these games from the market's own probabilities: **46.2 wins** on average, 95% of the time between **38 and 55**
- they actually won **44**
- **two-sided p = 0.651**
- that is **inside** the range, so the market has been right about them and any gap is ordinary variance

## Reading it

- **gap** is actual wins minus the wins the price implied. Near zero means the market had them right
- a flat-stake return near **−4% to −5%** is what a correctly priced team looks like — that is the hold, not a failing
- the buckets matter because a season-long gap of zero can be a team the market overrates as a favourite and underrates as a dog, which cancels in the total and is tradeable
- 82 games is one season. A gap needs to be large to mean anything at this sample size, which is what the simulated range is there to show
