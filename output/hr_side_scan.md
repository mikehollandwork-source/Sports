# Do winners homer more than losers?

_Every batter with a plate appearance in every board game. The hit props' 1.4-point lean to the losing side was noise (SE 8.2 points, p = 0.86); a home run is a different event because it SCORES, so the prior says winners should homer more._

- batter-games: **26141** over 99 dates

| side | batter-games | PA | HR | HR per PA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| team **WON** | 12811 | 49690 | 1917 | **3.86%** |
| team **LOST** | 13330 | 46645 | 1042 | **2.23%** |

- winners homer **+1.62 points per PA** more (a **1.73x** rate)
- standard error 0.11 points · z = +14.7 · **p = 0.00e+00**

## For contrast, the same split on HITS

- team won: **24.75%** hits per PA · team lost: **18.54%**
- difference **+6.20 points**, against +1.62 for home runs

_Hits barely separate the two sides; home runs separate them sharply, because a home run is part of what makes a team the winner._

## What this means for the board

- **Pick from the side you expect to WIN.** Taking the home run from the opposing lineup bets against a correlation this strong.

_This is a measurement of the BASE RATE, not of our selection. It says which side to draw from; it does not say the model picks the right bat, and the home-run book still has no graded entries._
