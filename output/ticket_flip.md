# When the tickets flip late and the money does not, who wins?

_On 2026-09-30 Boston/Yankees read "with public" from 04:05 to 21:03 — handle and tickets both on the Yankees, money climbing 60% to 76%. At 22:03 the TICKET majority flipped to Boston while the money stayed on the Yankees at 76%. Gate 1 failed and the game was passed on._

_A game counts as a late ticket flip when a pre-lock board said "with public", a later pre-lock board said "against public", **and the money side was the same in both** — which is what makes it the tickets moving rather than the money._

- boards with version history: **97** · graded games: **1042**

## Backing the MONEY side, by what the tickets did

| what happened | backing the money side | backing the other side |
|---|---|---|
| stayed agreed | 352-251 · **-0.8%** (n=603) | 251-352 · **-5.6%** (n=603) |
| ticket flip | 86-71 · **+2.2%** (n=157) | 71-86 · **-7.0%** (n=157) |
| money side moved | 59-57 · **-7.7%** (n=116) | 57-59 · **+0.8%** (n=116) |
| other | 86-80 · **-8.3%** (n=166) | 80-86 · **+5.3%** (n=166) |

## Is the flip population actually different?

- money side after a ticket flip: **+2.2%** (n=157)
- money side when they stayed agreed: **-0.8%** (n=603)
- difference **+2.9 pts** · shuffling the two groups gives ±15.3 · **p = 0.703**

- **not distinguishable.** The gate is sitting out a population it cannot show is worse — which is a cost, not a saving, if the money side still wins them

## Reading it

- sitting out is only correct if BOTH columns are bad. If the money side still pays after a ticket flip, the gate is discarding good bets
- this was found by watching one game, so it is an observation looking for support, not a tested rule
